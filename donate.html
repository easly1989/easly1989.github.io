Keep the free ones free.

I give some of what I build away whole — not a trial, not a free tier with the useful parts removed. Donating is what lets me keep making that choice.

The choice this pays for

Every app I release starts at the same fork: charge for it, or give it away. The commercial answer is always available and always tempting — put bank sync behind a subscription, cap the free plan at one account, sell the data, run ads. I would rather not, and a project that pays for nothing has to justify its own time.

Donations are what tip that decision. They do not buy features or priority; they widen the margin where giving the whole thing away is something I can afford to keep doing.

Where it goes

Time, mostly
The real cost of free software is not servers, it is evenings. Donations are what turn "when I get round to it" into fixed bugs, answered issues and releases that keep arriving.
Hosting that nobody is billed for
Planora is free and centrally hosted — I run the backend, so every group that signs up costs me capacity and no one is ever charged for it.
Features that stay ungated
Automatic bank sync, multi-user, two-factor auth, the full report suite: in a commercial product every one of those is a paid tier. In CloudBank they are just part of it, and I intend to keep it that way.

Why not just charge for everything?

Some of it I do. Nutreek and Cupid are licensed products with real customers, and that work is what currently subsidises the free work — the paid things quietly pay for the free things.

That is a workable arrangement, not a comfortable one: it means the free apps live on whatever time the paid ones leave over. Donations make that squeeze smaller, which is the whole of the pitch. If what you actually need is Nutreek or Cupid, don't donate — get in touch instead.
